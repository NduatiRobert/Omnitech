"How much does CCTV cost?" is the first question most people ask us. The honest answer is that it depends on a handful of decisions, and once you understand them you can plan a system that protects what matters without paying for things you don't need.

Here are the seven factors that have the biggest effect on the price of a CCTV installation in Kenya.

## 1. The number of cameras

This is the biggest driver of cost. Each camera adds hardware, cabling, a recorder channel and installation time. Before deciding on a number, list the areas you actually need to see:

- Entrances and exits (gates, doors, reception)
- Cash points, tills and stores
- Parking areas and perimeter walls
- Corridors and staircases in larger buildings

A good installer will help you cover these areas with the fewest cameras possible by choosing the right lens and placement.

## 2. Camera type and image quality

Cameras range from basic HD models to high-resolution IP cameras with colour night vision, audio and smart detection.

- **HD analogue (Turbo HD) cameras** are cost-effective and reliable for many homes and small shops.
- **IP cameras** give sharper images, more features and easier expansion, and suit offices, schools and larger sites.
- **PTZ cameras** can pan, tilt and zoom, and are useful for large open areas such as yards and parking lots.

Higher resolution is not always better. A well-placed 2MP or 4MP camera often gives more useful footage than a poorly placed 8MP one.

## 3. Indoor or outdoor installation

Outdoor cameras need weatherproof housings, better night vision and sometimes protective mounts. Long cable runs to gates or perimeter walls also add trunking, conduits and labour.

## 4. How long you want to keep footage

Footage is stored on a hard drive inside the DVR or NVR. The more cameras, the higher the quality, and the more days you want to keep, the bigger the drive you need. Most businesses keep 2 to 4 weeks of footage, which is usually enough to review any incident.

## 5. Power backup

Kenya's power interruptions are a real risk to security systems. A UPS or backup battery keeps cameras and the recorder running during blackouts. It adds to the upfront cost, but a system that switches off when the power goes off is not doing its job.

## 6. Remote viewing and smart features

Almost every modern system lets you watch live and recorded footage on your phone. Additional features, such as line-crossing alerts, face detection, people counting or viewing several branches from one app, may need specific cameras or recorders.

## 7. Installation quality and after-sales support

Neat, concealed cabling, correct camera angles and proper configuration make the difference between a system you trust and one you stop checking. Ask any installer:

1. Do you offer a free site survey?
2. Is the quotation itemised?
3. What warranty covers the equipment and the workmanship?
4. Do you offer maintenance after installation?

> **Tip:** The cheapest quotation often leaves out cabling accessories, power backup or storage. Compare itemised quotes, not just totals.
