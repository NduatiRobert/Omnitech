Affordable, reliable internet is still out of reach for many people in estates, hostels, markets and small towns. A well-run Wi-Fi hotspot fills that gap and can become a steady source of income. Here is how to get started the right way.

## Step 1: Choose the right location

Your location decides your customers. Look for places where many people stay or spend time and need data, for example:

- Residential estates and apartment blocks
- Student hostels and areas near colleges
- Markets, bus stages and shopping centres
- Cafés, restaurants and barbershops

Walk around at different times of the day and talk to potential users. How much do they spend on mobile data each week? Would they switch to cheaper Wi-Fi?

## Step 2: Get a reliable internet connection

Your hotspot is only as good as the internet behind it. Compare fibre and wireless options available in your area, and check:

- The speed you get compared with what is advertised
- How quickly the provider fixes outages
- Whether the package allows reselling or shared use

Start with a package that matches your expected number of users and upgrade as you grow.

## Step 3: Plan your equipment

A typical hotspot setup includes:

1. **A hotspot router or controller** that manages users and connects to your billing system.
2. **Access points or outdoor antennas** to spread coverage to where your customers are.
3. **Cabling, mounting and power backup** so the network stays online during blackouts.

Good planning matters more than expensive hardware. A coverage survey shows where to place access points so users get a strong signal without dead zones.

## Step 4: Set up automatic M-Pesa payments

Manual payments do not scale. A hotspot billing system lets customers:

- Open a branded login page on their phone
- Choose a package, such as 1 hour, 1 day, 1 week or 1 month
- Pay through an M-Pesa prompt and get connected automatically

The system also disconnects users when their package expires, so you earn while you sleep.

## Step 5: Price your packages

Set prices that are clearly cheaper than buying the same amount of mobile data, while still covering your internet bill, power and equipment costs. Common approaches:

| Package | Good for |
| --- | --- |
| Hourly | Visitors and occasional users |
| Daily | Students and people working from home |
| Weekly / monthly | Regular residents who want the best value |

Speed limits per package keep the network fair for everyone.

## Step 6: Market your hotspot

- Put clear posters at entrances, shops and stages with your Wi-Fi name and prices.
- Offer a short free trial so people can test the speed.
- Use WhatsApp groups and estate committees to spread the word.
- Reward loyal customers with bonus time.

## Step 7: Check regulatory requirements

Depending on the size and nature of your hotspot business, licensing or registration requirements may apply. Check the current requirements with the relevant authorities before you scale up.

## Grow step by step

Most successful hotspot owners start with one location, learn what their customers want, then add access points and new sites. With remote management, you can monitor users and sales across all of them from your phone.
